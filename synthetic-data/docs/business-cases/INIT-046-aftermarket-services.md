# Aftermarket Services Proposition — Outline Business Case

**Ref:** INIT-046
**Function:** Growth / Commercial
**Sponsor:** Chief Commercial Officer
**Lead:** Gregor Doherty
**Stage:** Business Case (not yet approved — targeting the November 2026 IC)

---

## The commercial logic

We sell equipment and then largely walk away. Our installed base is approximately 41,000
units across the industrial services and energy customers, and we capture service revenue on
fewer than a third of them. Competitors capture 55–70%. Every unit we do not service is a
unit somebody else services, and increasingly it is a unit somebody else *replaces*.

This initiative builds a productised aftermarket service catalogue — inspection, planned
maintenance, parts, and response SLAs — sold against the installed base.

Directly serves SO1 (grow profitable revenue in services and aftermarket), which is the
Board's first strategic objective.

## Sizing

Revenue uplift of **roughly $2.2m annually** once the catalogue is live and the sales motion
is established, though this is a planning estimate and I want to be honest that it is built
on an attach-rate assumption rather than on evidence. We assume we move from 31% to 42%
service attach on the addressable installed base over three years.

If the attach rate assumption is wrong by five points the benefit moves by about $900k in
either direction. Value confidence is recorded as **Low** for that reason.

Budget: $2,250,000 over about 13 months, starting February 2027.

Payback is long — this is not a quick win and should not be sold as one.

## What has to exist first

This is the part I would like the portfolio review to look at, because on our current plan
we start in February 2027 and several of our prerequisites are not obviously finished by then.

**Installed base data.** We cannot sell service against an installed base we cannot see.
Today the installed base is recorded in the legacy order management system, partially, and
in engineers' heads, mostly. The Master Data Management initiative is producing a single view
of installed base by customer. Without it, we are quoting blind.

**CRM.** Quoting, entitlement and contract management all run in the new CRM. There is no
version of this proposition that runs on the current sales tooling.

**Preventive maintenance standardisation.** This one is less obvious but more important. We
cannot sell a planned maintenance product if every region performs planned maintenance
differently — we would be selling an SLA we cannot consistently meet. Operations own that
work.

**Pricing.** The segmentation and elasticity model coming out of the pricing analytics work
is what sets the price points. We could price on cost-plus without it, and we would leave
money on the table.

And downstream of us: the Subscription Maintenance Offering is built directly on this
catalogue. They cannot define a subscription until there is something to subscribe to. Their
plan has them starting in January 2027, which is *before* we start. Somebody should look at
that.

## Risks

| Risk | P | I | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attach rate assumption unproven | 4 | 4 | Pilot in one region before committing |
| Field capacity to actually deliver the SLAs | 3 | 5 | Depends on Ops standardisation |
| Sales force not equipped to sell service | 4 | 3 | Needs enablement, not in this budget |
| Installed base data never materialises | 3 | 5 | Hard dependency on MDM |

## Open questions for the IC

1. Do we pilot in one region first (slower, safer, ~$400k) or launch group-wide?
2. Is the sales enablement funded from here or from the commercial function's own budget?
   Currently it is in neither.
3. Are we comfortable starting before the CRM and MDM dependencies are confirmed complete?
