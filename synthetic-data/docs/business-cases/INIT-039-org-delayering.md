BACKGROUND

Harbourline has nine management layers between the CEO and a frontline engineer. Peer
benchmarking puts the equivalent figure at six. Average span of control is 3.8; the target
operating model assumes 6.5. The consequence is a management population that is roughly
1.7x what the business needs, slow decision-making, and a cost base that carries about
$2.3m of avoidable management overhead.

This is the single largest people change in the transformation and it is politically the
most difficult. It is also, awkwardly, a prerequisite for several other things — see below.

WHAT WE WOULD DO

Three phases:

  Phase 1 — Diagnostic and target structure design (to Feb 2027)
  Phase 2 — Consultation and selection (to Aug 2027)
  Phase 3 — Implementation, wave by wave, by business unit (to mid-2028)

Consultation timelines are not negotiable and are the reason the end date is where it is.
The DACH and Benelux entities require works council engagement which historically has taken
between four and seven months. We have assumed six.

BENEFIT

  ~$2.3m per annum, possibly more.

That figure is management overhead removed. It excludes any productivity benefit from faster
decisions, which we believe is real but cannot evidence, and it excludes the cost to achieve
(redundancy, retention, consultation support) which is significant and is not yet in the
number. Finance have not signed this off. I would call the confidence LOW until they have.

  Budget: $2.41m
  Duration: about 22 months
  Benefit starts: after the last implementation wave, so FY29 for the full run rate

THINGS THAT DEPEND ON US (this is the important bit)

I want to flag this clearly because I do not think it is understood at portfolio level.

At least three other initiatives have designed their target operating model on the assumption
that the new span-of-control standard already exists:

  * The Finance Shared Services Centre is sizing its centre against the new structure. Their
    plan has them designing from August 2026. We do not publish a target structure until
    February 2027 at the earliest, and it is not implemented until 2028. If they design
    first, they design against today's org and will rework it.

  * The HR Shared Services operating model has the same problem, and it is worse because it
    is our own function and should have been sequenced properly in the first place.

  * The Contact Centre redesign inherits the span standard. Less critical — they could
    proceed on an assumed number and adjust — but they should know they are doing that.

I have raised this at the monthly PMO twice. The response both times was that the initiatives
would "run in parallel and align as they go". I do not think that is a plan.

WHAT WE DEPEND ON

Not much, which is part of the problem — we are early in the chain and everything else is
waiting on us. We need the HRIS consolidation to give us reliable establishment and reporting
line data, because the current org data is held in three systems and does not reconcile. As
at today the HRIS work is behind and I am told it is at risk.

RISKS

  - Works council consultation extends the timeline (probability: high, honestly)
  - Line managers are being asked to design their own delayering. Conflict of interest.
  - Attrition of good people during a long consultation window
  - Access provisioning delays are already slowing the diagnostic team down
  - The works council consultation in DACH has not been initiated and should have been

STATUS

Mobilisation gate was baselined for 10 September 2026. Current forecast is 25 October.
We have not formally started.
