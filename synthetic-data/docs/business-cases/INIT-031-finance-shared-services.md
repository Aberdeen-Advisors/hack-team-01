## 1. Executive summary

We propose to consolidate transactional finance activity from eleven locations into a single
shared service centre. Today the same accounts payable process is performed eleven different
ways by 94 FTE, with no common system of record and a month-end close that takes nine working
days.

The centre would take on AP, AR, cash application, intercompany and fixed assets in phase 1.
Payroll and tax stay with the local entities for now — this was debated at length and the
decision was to descope payroll rather than delay the whole thing.

Expected saving is **in the region of $0.9m to $1.1m annually once fully ramped**, driven
mainly by headcount reduction and a lower cost-to-serve per transaction. We have not yet
validated the upper end of that range with Finance. Treat $990k as the planning number.

## 2. Strategic fit

Serves SO2 (simplify and standardise) primarily and SO3 (cost out) secondarily. The Group
Financial Controller considers this the single most visible proof point that the operating
model is actually changing.

## 3. Scope

**In scope:** AP, AR, cash application, intercompany reconciliation, fixed asset accounting.
Eleven source locations. UK & Ireland and Benelux entities in wave 1.

**Out of scope:** payroll, tax, treasury, statutory reporting, anything in North America
(different regulatory regime, revisit FY28).

**Explicitly undecided:** whether the centre is captive or outsourced. The business case
assumes captive. If the Board prefers a BPO model the numbers change materially and this
case would need to be rewritten.

## 4. Dependencies and sequencing

This is the section that has caused the most argument, so it is worth being blunt.

**We cannot stand this centre up on the current ledgers.** The whole saving depends on
processing one standard transaction set, and that does not exist until the ERP finance module
consolidation has landed. The ERP team's current view is a June 2027 finish. Our plan assumes
we can start building the centre in parallel from August 2026 and be ready to receive
processes shortly after ERP go-live. That parallel-running assumption is doing a lot of work
and has not been stress-tested.

**Second, and more awkwardly** — the target headcount for the centre assumes the new
span-of-control standard from the organisational delayering work. If delayering lands after
we have designed the centre, we will have designed it around the old structure and will have
to do it twice. The delayering programme's own plan currently runs into mid-2028. Nobody has
reconciled these two dates. I have raised this three times.

Also relevant, though less critical:
- The transactional volume we are sizing for assumes the RPA factory has already stripped out
  the high-volume, low-value work. If RPA slips, our headcount target is wrong (too low).
- Order-to-cash automation should really be complete before O2C transfers in, otherwise we are
  transferring a manual process into a new building and calling it transformation.
- Internal Audit have said, in writing, that they will not sign off the transfer of any
  process with an open material control deficiency against it. That points at the SOX
  remediation work.
- Establishment data for the resourcing model comes out of the new HRIS.

## 6. Benefits

Benefit type: Cost Save. Confidence: **Medium**.

Savings land after the last process transfers, not before. There is a natural dip in the
first two quarters as we run duplicate capacity during transition — the business case shows
this as a cost, but the Board deck does not, which has caused confusion.

Value confidence is Medium rather than High because:
- the headcount reduction has not been agreed with HR or the works councils;
- the saving is partly dependent on initiatives outside our control (see §4);
- the FY25 baseline for cost-to-serve was calculated by the previous FP&A team and we have
  not been able to reproduce it.

## 8. Risks

Top three as we see them:
- Vendor commercial dispute — currently live, and it is holding up the next phase.
- Works council consultation in the Benelux entities has not started.
- Key SME is shared with two other initiatives and is not formally allocated to us.

## 9. What we need

Decision on captive vs BPO. A single answer on the delayering timeline. And someone to own
the question of what happens if ERP slips again.
