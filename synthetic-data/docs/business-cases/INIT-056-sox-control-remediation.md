# SOX Control Remediation (INIT-056)

Risk & Compliance | Sponsor: Group General Counsel | Lead: Zoya Halvorsen
Approved — mandatory, not discretionary
Rev 3, 22 June 2026

---

## Mandate

Following the FY25 external audit, six material control deficiencies and seventeen
significant deficiencies were raised across the financial reporting control environment.
The Audit Committee has committed to the external auditor that all six material deficiencies
will be remediated before the FY27 year-end testing cycle.

**This initiative is not optional and is not available for trade-off.** It is flagged
`is_regulatory = TRUE` and under the Board's stated position (strategy one-pager, SO4) it is
funded ahead of discretionary work in every scenario.

## Scope

The six material deficiencies:

1. Segregation of duties in the purchase-to-pay cycle (four entities)
2. Access recertification not performed to schedule for financially significant systems
3. Journal entry approval thresholds inconsistently applied
4. IT general controls — change management evidence not retained
5. Revenue recognition review controls not operating in the Energy Solutions entity
6. Manual balance sheet reconciliation controls without independent review

Plus a control rationalisation exercise that reduces the tested control population from
approximately 480 to a target of ~310 by removing duplicated and redundant controls.

## Benefit

This is the section that always causes trouble at portfolio review.

Recorded annual benefit: **$450,000**, benefit type Risk Reduction, confidence Low.

That figure is external audit fee reduction plus the internal effort saved by testing 310
controls instead of 480. It is real but it is small, and it is *not the point*. The point is
that a material weakness disclosed in the annual report is a governance event with
consequences that do not appear on a benefits tracker.

Every time this portfolio is ranked on financial return, this initiative appears near the
bottom. It should not be cut. It cannot be cut.

## Dependencies

**Upstream:** the access-control remediation (deficiency 2) depends on the modernised role
model coming out of the Identity & Access Management work. We cannot recertify access against
a role model that does not exist. IAM is currently forecast to complete October 2026 and we
have planned around that.

**Downstream — and this is the one people miss:** Internal Audit's stated position is that no
process may transfer into the Finance Shared Services Centre while it carries an open material
deficiency against it. Purchase-to-pay and balance sheet reconciliation are both in scope for
the SSC transfer and both carry open deficiencies. In practice this means SSC process transfer
is gated on our completion. The SSC business case acknowledges this in passing. Their plan
does not appear to reflect it.

## Principal risks

- Audit findings require rework of the control design — if the external auditor does not
  accept the remediated design at walkthrough, we redo it with no time left.
- Control evidence not retained to the required standard (this is deficiency 4 recurring as
  a delivery risk, which is uncomfortable but accurate).
- Compliance specialist capacity — we have 1.5 FTE of a specialist skill that the Data Privacy
  programme also needs.

## Ask

Confirmation that the FY27 testing cycle date is fixed. If it moves left, we have a problem
we cannot solve with money.
