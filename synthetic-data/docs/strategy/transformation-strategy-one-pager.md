# Harbourline Group — Transformation Strategy on a Page
**FY26–FY29 | Approved by the Board, 18 November 2025 | Owner: Group Strategy**

> Reporting currency is USD. All figures are group-level unless stated.

---

## Where we are

Harbourline Group is a $2.1bn industrial services and distribution business operating across
five business units and six regions. Three acquisitions in four years have left us with
duplicated systems, inconsistent processes and a cost base that has grown faster than revenue.
Margin has compressed 180bps since FY23. We run four ERPs, three HR systems and eleven
warehouse management instances.

We have no shortage of initiatives. We have a shortage of sequence.

---

## The four strategic objectives

### SO1 — Grow profitable revenue in services and aftermarket
Shift the revenue mix away from one-off product sales towards recurring service income.

| Measure | Baseline (FY25) | Target (FY29) |
|---|---|---|
| Services share of revenue | 22% | 35% |
| Recurring / contracted revenue | $310m | $520m |
| Bid win rate | 26% | 34% |

### SO2 — Simplify and standardise how we operate
One way of working per process, on one platform per capability. This is the objective that
most of the enabling technology investment serves.

| Measure | Baseline (FY25) | Target (FY29) |
|---|---|---|
| ERP instances | 4 | 1 |
| HR systems | 3 | 1 |
| Documented standard processes (of 40 core) | 11 | 40 |
| Month-end close (working days) | 9 | 4 |

### SO3 — Take $25m out of the cost base, sustainably
Structural cost, not one-off cuts. Savings must be validated by Finance and visible in the
run rate, not just in a slide.

| Measure | Baseline (FY25) | Target (FY29) |
|---|---|---|
| Run-rate cost reduction | — | $25m |
| Indirect spend under management | 41% | 85% |
| Supplier count (indirect) | ~4,100 | ~1,600 |

### SO4 — Protect the licence to operate
Non-negotiable. Regulatory and control work is funded first and is not available for
trade-off in any scenario.

| Measure | Baseline (FY25) | Target (FY29) |
|---|---|---|
| Open high-severity audit findings | 23 | 0 |
| Sites on the standard H&S reporting platform | 34% | 100% |
| Material SOX control deficiencies | 6 | 0 |

## How initiatives are scored against this

Every initiative carries a `strategic_alignment` score of 1–5 reflecting how directly it
serves one of the four objectives above. Regulatory initiatives (SO4) are floored at 4.
The `pillar` field on each initiative records which value theme it serves:

| Value theme (`pillar`) | Serves | Plain meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Grow** | SO1 | New or better revenue |
| **Run Better** | SO2 | Simpler, faster, more standard |
| **Cost Out** | SO3 | Structural cost removed |
| **Protect** | SO4 | Risk, control, compliance |

The `function` field records *who owns delivery* — Technology, Operations, Supply Chain,
Finance, HR / People, Growth / Commercial, Cost Reduction, Risk & Compliance. The two are
deliberately different lenses: a Technology-owned initiative can serve a Cost Out theme, and
frequently does.

---

## The four things the Board has said out loud

1. **Regulatory work is protected.** SO4 initiatives are funded ahead of everything else.
2. **We will not fund everything.** The current portfolio ask exceeds the envelope.
3. **Benefits must be validated by Finance** before they are counted. Sponsor optimism is
   not evidence.
4. **We want to see value inside twelve months.** The Board has been patient for two years
   and is no longer patient.

---

## Known tensions (unresolved, deliberately)

- SO1 (grow) and SO3 (cost out) compete for the same commercial and change capacity.
- SO2 (simplify) is mostly *enabling* — it produces little standalone benefit but gates a
  large share of the SO1 and SO3 value. Cutting it looks cheap and is not.
- Nobody has yet agreed whether the $25m in SO3 is gross or net of the cost to achieve it.

*Note from Group Strategy, July 2026: the incoming Transformation Director has asked for an
integrated view of how the current 60 initiatives actually ladder up to these four objectives.
As at the date of this note, no such view exists.*
